Background on Liquid Crystals

  • Smectic: orientational order, translational order in 1 dimension (called z)
  • Nematic: orientational order, no translational order (behaves as liquid in 3 dimensions)
  • Isotropic: No order, just liquid
  • Birefringence: Light is broken up into rays of different velocities by molecular structure
  • Racemic: even mixture of right and left handed molecules
  • Chiral: all molecules either right or left handed
  • Smectic C: all molecules oriented in the same direction, tilted with respect to the crystal direction z
    • difference between molecular orientation n and smectic direction z is a 2d vector field called the c-director
  • Smectic C liquid crystals have point defects, singularities of the c-director field
    • places where the director field spontaneously swaps direction
    • +1 defects are called sources and exist inside islands
    • -1 defects are called curls and exist outside of islands
  • Smectic C defects form equilibrium structures
    • Quadrupole: between a right handed island and left handed island, 2 +1 defects and 2 -1 defects forming a cross. Not present in chiral materials.
    • Dipole chains: repeating +1 -1 defects, islands chained together with -1 vortices in between. Can exist in both chiral and racemic materials
  • My project will be to collect data of these defects under polarized microscopy, and optimize a machine learning model to detect these defects, and model the interactions between +1 and -1 defects.
